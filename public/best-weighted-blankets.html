How to choose

Start at about 10% of your body weight

Sleep Foundation, Tom's Guide, NBC Select and TechRadar all suggest roughly 10% of your body weight: a 150 lb adult would pick about a 15 lb blanket, and Tom's Guide says about 20 lb suits someone around 220 lb. Most adults land between 10 and 20 lb. If you're between sizes, go lighter; Reviewed's testers found 25 lb felt suffocating.

Size it to your body, not your mattress

A single weighted blanket should rest on top of you rather than drape over the sides of the bed, per Tom's Guide; a 48x72-inch blanket suits one adult. Couples who want to share need a queen or king size, like the 86x92-inch Quility or Saatva, or the queen-size 20 and 25 lb Layla, or two separate blankets, since partners may need different weights.

Hot sleepers: pick an open knit or a lyocell cover

Chunky knits like the Bearaby and Silk & Snow, and cool-to-the-touch lyocell or bamboo covers like the Luxome's, breathe best, per Sleep Foundation and Mattress Clarity. Thick minky and micro-plush covers, like the Gravity's, hold more heat.

Check how you'll wash it

Many washers top out around 20 lb, Reviewed warns, so a 20 or 25 lb blanket may need a commercial machine. Blankets with a removable cover (Luxome, Gravity) are easiest: wash the cover often and the insert rarely. Spot-clean-only models are the hardest to keep fresh, per Tom's Guide.

Prefer glass beads and tight stitching

Sleep Foundation says glass beads and thick fabric hold up better than cheap plastic pellets. Small sewn pockets, like the Baloo's double-stitched baffles and the Layla's hexagon quilting, keep the beads from sliding to one end.

Look for a trial and a warranty

Return windows range from 30 days to 100 nights (Silk & Snow) and 120 nights (Layla, per Mattress Clarity). Warranties range from 1 year to lifetime (Baloo). Some brands charge a return fee, so check before you buy.

Questions people ask

What is the best weighted blanket in 2026?

The Bearaby Cotton Weighted Blanket is the best weighted blanket in 2026 for most people: it's Sleep Foundation's top pick and NBC Select's coziest, with a breathable organic cotton knit. For a lower price, the YnM Weighted Blanket is NBC Select's most affordable pick and Reviewed's best value.

How heavy should a weighted blanket be?

A weighted blanket should weigh about 10% of your body weight, the guideline Sleep Foundation, Tom's Guide and NBC Select all give: about 15 lb for a 150 lb person and 20 lb for someone around 200–220 lb. If you're unsure, choose the lighter option.

Are weighted blankets worth it?

Weighted blankets are worth it if you like the feeling of gentle, even pressure, but the science is limited: Sleep Foundation calls the evidence fairly inconclusive, and NBC Select's experts say results vary from person to person. Small studies suggest they may help some people feel calmer and sleep better, but they don't treat insomnia or anxiety.

What is the best weighted blanket for hot sleepers?

The best weighted blankets for hot sleepers are open-knit or cooling-cover designs: the Bearaby Cotton Weighted Blanket and Silk & Snow's knit let air through, and the Luxome Removable Cover Weighted Blanket is Sleep Foundation's best cooling pick thanks to its lyocell cover.

Can you put a weighted blanket in the washing machine?

Many weighted blankets can go in the washing machine, including the Bearaby, Baloo, Layla and Luna Luxe, but heavy ones can overload a home washer. Reviewed notes most washers top out around 20 lb, and Baloo suggests a commercial machine for its 25 lb size. Others, like the Gravity Blanket, have a washable cover but a hand-wash-only insert.

Who should not use a weighted blanket?

People with breathing problems such as asthma or sleep apnea, circulation or blood pressure issues, type 2 diabetes or claustrophobia should talk to a doctor before using a weighted blanket, per Tom's Guide and Sleep Foundation. They're also not for babies or toddlers, and children should never use an adult-weight blanket.
