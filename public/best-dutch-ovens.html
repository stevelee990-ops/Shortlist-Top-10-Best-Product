How to choose

Buy 5 to 7 quarts for most households

Forbes Vetted and Food Network say a 5- to 7-quart pot suits most cooks. Reviewed's sizing guide suggests 3 to 4 quarts for one or two people and 5 to 7 quarts for a family of four; go 7 quarts or larger if you cook for a crowd or want a whole chicken to fit. NBC Select's expert recommends buying bigger, since a large pot can cook small portions but not the other way around.

Look for a wide base and straight sides

America's Test Kitchen recommends a pot at least 8 inches across, and Food Network prefers straight sides: sloped sides, like the Lodge 6 Quart's, leave less room to sear meat without crowding.

Choose a light interior to see browning

Cream or white enamel, as in the Le Creuset, Tramontina Bestow and Cuisinart, makes it easier to tell when onions or fond are browning. Dark interiors like the Staub's hide stains but make browning harder to judge, per Forbes Vetted and Food Network.

Check the knob's oven rating before baking bread

Bread recipes often call for a 450–500°F oven. Le Creuset's black phenolic knob is rated to 500°F and its stainless knob has no limit, per Food Network; the Tramontina Bestow tops out at 450°F. BestReviews notes plastic knobs don't hold up to broiling.

Lift-test the weight

Enameled cast iron Dutch ovens here weigh about 11 to 16 lb empty, and Forbes Vetted warns a full pot can be much heavier. If that's too much, the Caraway Dutch Oven weighs about 6 lb, and America's Test Kitchen suggests a stainless steel stockpot as a lighter alternative.

Pick enameled over bare cast iron for easy care

Enamel needs no seasoning and can handle acidic foods like tomato sauce, while bare cast iron needs regular seasoning, per NBC Select and BestReviews. Hand-wash with a soft sponge even if the label says dishwasher-safe, skip steel wool, and use a baking soda paste or Bar Keepers Friend for stains.

Questions people ask

What is the best Dutch oven in 2026?

The Le Creuset Signature Round Dutch Oven is the best Dutch oven in 2026: it's the top pick at Food Network, Forbes Vetted and NBC Select, and America's Test Kitchen calls it the gold standard. For a much lower price, the Lodge 6 Quart Enameled Cast Iron Dutch Oven is Forbes Vetted's best value pick.

Is a Le Creuset Dutch oven worth it?

A Le Creuset Dutch oven is worth it if you cook stews, braises and bread often and want a pot that lasts decades: it aced testing at Food Network and Forbes Vetted, is made in France and has a lifetime warranty. If you cook with one only occasionally, the Lodge 6 Quart and Tramontina Bestow come close on heat retention and roasting results for far less, per Forbes Vetted.

What size Dutch oven should I buy?

Most people should buy a 5- to 7-quart Dutch oven, the size Forbes Vetted and Food Network recommend for most home cooks. Choose about 5 quarts or smaller for one or two people, and 7 quarts or more for big families or frequent hosting.

Is Staub or Le Creuset better?

Le Creuset is better for most cooks: Forbes Vetted ranked it first and the Staub second, because the Le Creuset's light interior makes browning easier to see and it's about 2 lb lighter with bigger handles. Choose the Staub if you want its self-basting lid spikes and a dark interior that hides stains.

What is as good as Le Creuset but cheaper?

The Lodge 6 Quart Enameled Cast Iron Dutch Oven and the Tramontina Bestow are the closest lower-cost matches to Le Creuset, according to Forbes Vetted's testing. America's Test Kitchen's affordable pick, the Cuisinart 7-Quart Round Covered Casserole, has a very similar design for about a third of the price.

Should I get an enameled or regular cast iron Dutch oven?

Get an enameled cast iron Dutch oven for everyday cooking: the enamel needs no seasoning, cleans easily and can handle acidic foods like tomato sauce. Bare cast iron holds heat just as well and costs less, but it needs regular seasoning, per NBC Select and BestReviews, and BestReviews says enameled pots shouldn't go over an open campfire.
