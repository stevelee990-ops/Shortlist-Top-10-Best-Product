How to choose

Decide between a bed blanket and a throw

Electric blankets come in twin to king sizes for sleeping, while heated throws (usually about 50 x 60 inches) are for the couch or desk. HGTV notes most models are made for one use or the other, so pick the size for where you'll actually use it.

Look for a safety certification and auto shut-off

Every pick here carries a UL or ETL mark from an independent testing lab, which fire-safety experts quoted by NBC Select recommend. An auto shut-off timer (from 2 to 12 hours here) and overheat protection keep a forgotten blanket from staying on.

Couples should get dual controls

On queen and king beds, dual-control blankets such as the Serta Fleece to Sherpa, Beautyrest Microfleece and Shavel Thermee let each person set their own side. Sleep Foundation notes larger blankets often have two heating zones.

More heat settings means finer control

Budget throws often have three or four settings; the Beautyrest Microfleece has 20 (about 85°F to 105°F). Sleep Foundation says cheaper models usually have fewer settings and premium ones add programmable timers and preheat.

Pick the fabric for the feel you want

Most electric blankets are polyester flannel, fleece or sherpa. NBC Select warns thin polyester can let you feel the wires, and BestReviews notes thicker fabrics still keep you warm when the power is off.

Check the cord length and outlet

Measure the distance to your outlet: several picks have short cords (the GOTCOZY's is about 4 feet), while the Shavel Thermee's are very long. Plug the blanket straight into a wall outlet, never a power strip or extension cord, as HGTV and BestReviews advise.

Questions people ask

Is it safe to sleep with an electric blanket on all night?

Fire-safety experts quoted by NBC Select advise turning an electric blanket off before you fall asleep; a safer habit is to preheat the bed and then switch it off. If you do use one overnight, Sleep Foundation says to choose a model with auto shut-off (most bed blankets here stop after about 10 hours), keep it flat and never lie on top of it.

Are electric blankets safe?

Yes, modern electric blankets are generally safe for most adults when used as directed, according to NBC Select and HGTV: buy a new one with a UL or ETL mark and auto shut-off, plug it into a wall outlet, don't bunch it up while it's on and never use it while it's damp. Don't use them for infants or anyone who can't operate the control, and NBC Select's pediatric expert advises against use during pregnancy.

How much does it cost to run an electric blanket?

Usually about 10 cents a night or less: Sleep Foundation estimates a typical blanket uses 40 to 60 watts on medium, and a 100-watt blanket running for eight hours uses about 0.8 kWh, or roughly $0.10 at $0.13 per kWh. Dual-zone models can draw 100 to 200 watts.

Can you wash an electric blanket?

Most electric blankets are machine washable once you unplug and remove the controller, but always follow the care label. HGTV describes a typical routine: a short gentle cycle, a brief tumble dry, then air dry fully before plugging it back in; electric blankets usually can't be dry cleaned.

What's the difference between an electric blanket and a heated throw?

An electric blanket is sized to fit a bed (twin to king) and often has a longer timer and dual controls for sleeping, while a heated throw is a smaller blanket (about 50 x 60 inches) for the couch with a shorter 2- to 4-hour shut-off. HGTV notes most models are designed for one use or the other.

How long do electric blankets last?

Most electric blankets last at least five years, according to Sleep Foundation, though heavy nightly use shortens that. Inspect the cord and wiring regularly and replace a blanket that's damaged, and don't buy used or vintage models, as BestReviews advises.
