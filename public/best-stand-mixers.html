How to choose

Tilt-head or bowl-lift?

Tilt-head mixers like the KitchenAid Artisan are easier to use and usually cheaper, and are ideal for cookies, cakes and single batches; check that your cabinets clear the head when it's raised. Bowl-lift mixers such as the KitchenAid 7-Quart have wider bases and bigger bowls for large, heavy batches.

A 5-quart bowl suits most kitchens

Food Network calls 5 quarts a good size for home baking; BestReviews suggests 4 to 4.5 quarts for one or two people and about 6 quarts for a large family. America's Test Kitchen measured usable capacity well below the label on many mixers, so size up if you bake double batches.

Torque matters more than watts

America's Test Kitchen calls torque the true measure of a stand mixer: a good mixer doesn't slow down or strain in heavy dough. The 325-watt KitchenAid Artisan and 500-watt Cuisinart SM-50 show that wattage alone doesn't predict results.

Bake bread often? Check the kneading rules

KitchenAid recommends kneading on speed 2, which ATK found makes heavy dough slower. Mixers with no kneading speed limit, like the Ankarsrum Assistent Original, or a spiral hook, like the Ooni Halo Pro, are built for frequent bread and pizza dough.

Look at the attachments

At minimum you want a flat beater, dough hook and wire whisk. A flex-edge beater saves scraping, a pouring shield cuts splatter, and ATK prefers uncoated stainless steel tools because coated ones can chip.

Heavier is steadier

Light, cheap mixers can shake or walk across the counter with stiff dough. If you'll store the mixer in a cupboard, weigh that against how often you'll lift it: the KitchenAid 7-Quart weighs about 25.6 lb, the Bosch Universal Plus about 12.7 lb (ATK).

Questions people ask

What is the best stand mixer in 2026?

The KitchenAid Artisan Stand Mixer (5-quart, KSM150) is the best stand mixer for most people in 2026: it's the best overall pick at Food Network, Tom's Guide and BestReviews. For less money, the Cuisinart Precision Master SM-50 is Tom's Guide's best budget pick, with a 5.5-quart bowl and a 500-watt motor.

Is a KitchenAid stand mixer worth it?

A KitchenAid is worth it if you bake regularly: the Artisan handles cookie dough, egg whites and whipped cream reliably, and its hub takes optional attachments like pasta makers. To save money, America's Test Kitchen found the KitchenAid Classic 4.5-quart performs similarly for about $150 less and named it its Best Buy.

Which is better, a tilt-head or bowl-lift stand mixer?

A tilt-head mixer is better for most home bakers because it's easier to use, usually cheaper and fine for cookies and cakes. A bowl-lift mixer is better if you often make large or heavy batches, because it has a wider base and a bigger bowl.

What size stand mixer do I need?

A 5-quart stand mixer is the right size for most home bakers, according to Food Network. BestReviews suggests 4 to 4.5 quarts for one or two people and around 6 quarts for a large family; a 3.5-quart mini like the KitchenAid Artisan Mini suits small kitchens but can't handle double batches.

What is the best stand mixer for bread dough?

The Ankarsrum Assistent Original is the best stand mixer for bread dough in America's Test Kitchen's testing: it aced pizza and bagel doughs and has no speed limit for kneading. Tom's Guide's pick for bread is the Ooni Halo Pro Spiral Mixer, whose spiral hook gives excellent gluten development in large batches.

How many watts should a stand mixer have?

Wattage isn't a reliable guide to stand mixer performance: America's Test Kitchen says torque is what counts, and BestReviews notes a well-engineered lower-wattage mixer can beat a cheaper high-wattage one. For example, the 325-watt KitchenAid Artisan is a top pick over many more powerful models.
