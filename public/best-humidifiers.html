How to choose

Match the tank to the room

HGTV suggests a tank under 3 liters for rooms below about 200 sq ft and roughly 6 liters for spaces around 500 sq ft. Open doors, high ceilings and drafty rooms need more, so buy a unit rated for a little more space than you have.

Ultrasonic or evaporative?

Ultrasonic models are the quietest and put out plenty of mist, but minerals in tap water can settle as white dust. Evaporative models blow air through a wet wick, leave little residue and handle tap water, but the wick needs replacing every few months.

Warm mist or cool mist?

Warm mist can feel soothing in cold weather, but the water gets very hot, so cool mist is the safer choice around kids and pets. Mist temperature barely changes the temperature of the room.

Get a humidistat or auto mode

Aim for 30–50% indoor humidity (the EPA says stay below 60%). A built-in humidity sensor or auto mode stops the mist once it hits your target, which saves water and helps prevent mold; without one, use an inexpensive hygrometer.

Plan for cleaning

Rinse and refill the tank daily and clean it at least weekly, usually with white vinegar (check the manual). Top-fill tanks, wide openings and dishwasher-safe parts make this far easier, and distilled water or a demineralization cartridge cuts mineral buildup.

Questions people ask

Is a warm mist or cool mist humidifier better?

Cool mist is the better choice for most homes, especially with kids or pets, because warm-mist models heat the water until it's very hot. HGTV and Bob Vila say warm mist can feel nicer in cold climates and may ease winter congestion, and Bob Vila notes mist temperature rarely changes the room's temperature.

What size humidifier do I need?

Match the tank and coverage rating to the room: HGTV suggests a tank under 3 liters for spaces under 200 sq ft and about 6 liters for roughly 500 sq ft. For open-plan areas or a whole floor, look at large evaporative models such as the Levoit Superior 6000S (up to 3,000 sq ft) or the Vornado Evap40 (up to 1,000 sq ft).

Should I use distilled water in a humidifier?

Use distilled water in ultrasonic humidifiers if you see white dust, because minerals in tap water come out with the mist and settle on nearby surfaces. Evaporative humidifiers trap minerals in their wick, so tap water is fine; Bob Vila says the Levoit Superior 6000S works with tap water for that reason.

What humidity should my house be in winter?

Keep indoor humidity between 30% and 50%. Live Science cites EPA guidance to stay below 60%, and Bob Vila recommends staying under 50% to limit mold and mildew, so a humidifier with a humidistat or auto mode is worth having.

Where should I put a humidifier in my bedroom?

Put it at least 3 ft from the bed, at least 1 ft from walls, furniture and curtains, and about 1 ft off the floor, with the mist pointed toward the middle of the room, as Live Science advises. A quiet ultrasonic or evaporative model with a sleep mode works best at night.

Is an ultrasonic or evaporative humidifier better?

Ultrasonic humidifiers are quieter and need less upkeep, which is why HGTV's top pick is ultrasonic; evaporative humidifiers avoid white dust and are happy with tap water but need replacement wicks. Bob Vila adds that ultrasonic units push out more moisture, while evaporative ones need regular cleaning to keep bacteria away.
