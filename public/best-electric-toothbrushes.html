How to choose

Get a pressure sensor and a timer

Dentists quoted by CNN Underscored call a pressure sensor helpful, because brushing too hard can wear enamel and cause gum recession. A two-minute timer with 30-second quadrant alerts keeps you brushing as long as the ADA recommends: two minutes, twice a day. TechRadar says these two features matter more than premium extras.

Sonic or oscillating: pick what you like

Sonic brushes such as the Sonicare 4100 and Suri 2.0 vibrate at up to about 40,000 movements per minute; oscillating-rotating Oral-B brushes turn a small round head up to about 8,000 times per minute, per TechRadar. NBC Select's experts say both clean equally well.

Budget for brush heads

Heads need replacing every three to four months, so check their price: NBC Select lists about $27 for three Sonicare 4100 heads and about $38 for two Oral-B iO heads. Generic heads are often cheaper, but check they fit your handle.

Look for soft bristles and the ADA Seal

Soft bristles are safer for teeth and gums. The ADA Seal of Acceptance (on the Oral-B Pro 1000 and Sonicare 4100, per NBC Select) shows a brand submitted safety and effectiveness evidence; it's voluntary, so a brush without it isn't necessarily worse.

Match battery life and charging to your travel

Battery life ranges from about 10 days (the Oral-B Pro 1000 in Electric Teeth's test) to 38 days or more (the Suri 2.0). If you travel, look for a case and USB-C charging, which Tom's Guide finds more flexible than a dock that needs a wall outlet.

Questions people ask

Are electric toothbrushes better than manual ones?

For most people, yes: electric brushes generally remove more plaque and reduce gum inflammation, according to TechRadar, and NBC Select's experts note they need less technique. A manual brush used with good technique for two minutes still works.

Is an electric toothbrush worth it?

Yes, if you'll use it twice a day, but you don't need a premium model. TechRadar says that once you pick a reputable brand, extra money mostly buys convenience, so a brush with a timer and pressure sensor, such as the Oral-B Pro 1000 or Suri Core 1.0, covers the essentials. Remember to budget for replacement heads.

Is Oral-B or Sonicare better?

Neither is clearly better. Oral-B uses a small round oscillating-rotating head, while Sonicare brushes vibrate at high speed; one 12-week study favored oscillating brushes, but the dentist TechRadar interviewed says the evidence isn't conclusive, and NBC Select's experts say both types clean equally well. Choose the one you'll use consistently.

How often should you replace an electric toothbrush head?

Every three to four months, as the ADA recommends, or sooner if the bristles look frayed or splayed. Tom's Guide adds that people with conditions such as periodontal disease may need to replace heads more often.

What's the best electric toothbrush for sensitive teeth?

The Philips Sonicare 7100 is Tom's Guide's pick for sensitive teeth, thanks to its Sensitive mode, three intensities and pressure alert; NBC Select picks the cheaper Oral-B Pro 1000. If your gums bleed, NBC Select's experts suggest seeing a dentist first, since bleeding often signals inflammation.

How long does an electric toothbrush last?

The handle typically lasts up to about five years, according to Tom's Guide, depending on battery health, charging habits, water exposure and drops. Brush heads should be swapped every three to four months.
