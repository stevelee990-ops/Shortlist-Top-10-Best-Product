How to choose

Brightness matters most

Look for ANSI lumens. Around 1,500+ is fine for a dark room; 2,500–3,500+ is better if you'll watch with lights on or daylight.

Know your throw type

Long-throw projectors sit at the back of the room. Ultra-short-throw (UST) models sit inches from the wall on a TV stand. Portables go anywhere.

The screen makes a difference

A plain white wall works, but an ambient-light-rejecting (ALR) screen greatly improves contrast — especially for UST projectors.

Gaming? Check input lag

For consoles, look for low input lag and a dedicated game mode. Models marked for gaming feel much more responsive.

Questions people ask

Is a projector better than a TV?

For the biggest picture per dollar, yes. For bright rooms and everyday convenience, a big TV is often easier.

Do I need a screen?

No, but it helps. A smooth, white or light grey wall is a fine start; a dedicated screen boosts contrast and brightness.

What about lamp replacement?

Most models on this list use laser or LED light that lasts many thousands of hours, so there are no lamps to replace.
