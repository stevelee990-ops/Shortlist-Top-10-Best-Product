How to choose

Pick the bookstore before the device

Yahoo Tech says the ecosystem matters most: Kindle books you buy from Amazon only read on Kindles and Kindle apps, and Kobo has its own store. If you already own Kindle books, stay with a Kindle; if you mostly borrow from the library or sideload EPUB files, a Kobo is easier.

Choose the screen size for how you read

6-inch models like the Amazon Kindle (2026) (about 140 g) and Kobo Clara BW (174 g) slip into a jacket pocket; 7-inch models like the Kindle Paperwhite (about 204 g) show more words per page; 11-inch models like the Kindle Scribe weigh about 400 g and are best on a desk or lap.

Get IPX8 if you read in the bath or by the pool

The basic Kindle isn't water resistant, but the Kindle Paperwhite, Colorsoft, Kobo Clara BW, Clara Colour and Libra Colour are all rated IPX8. The Kindle Scribe Colorsoft has no waterproofing.

Check for warm light and page buttons

Warm light shifts the screen toward amber for night reading; the basic Kindle doesn't have it. Among the Kindles and Kobos here, only the Kobo Libra Colour has physical page-turn buttons; for a Kindle, Amazon sells a $79.99 Page-Turn Cover and a $34.99 Kindle Click Bluetooth remote.

Only pay for color if you'll use it

Color E Ink shows color at 150 ppi, half the sharpness of text, and looks muted next to a tablet, per Tom's Guide and Yahoo Tech. It's worth it for comics, graphic novels and cookbooks; for novels, a black-and-white Kindle Paperwhite is cheaper.

Read battery claims as light use

Tom's Guide says Amazon's battery figures assume about 30 minutes of reading a day at low brightness, and Yahoo Tech notes audiobooks drain the battery faster. 16GB still holds thousands of ebooks, but audiobook listeners should look at 32GB models.

Questions people ask

What is the best e-reader in 2026?

The Kindle Paperwhite is the best e-reader for most people in 2026: it's the top pick at Tom's Guide, TechRadar, Forbes Vetted and Yahoo Tech, with a 7-inch, 300 ppi screen, warm light, IPX8 waterproofing and up to 12 weeks of battery for $199.99. On a tighter budget, the $149.99 Amazon Kindle (2026) is NBC Select's top pick.

Is Kindle or Kobo better?

Kindle is better if you buy ebooks from Amazon, and Kobo is better if you borrow library books or load your own files. Forbes Vetted says Kindle is the more seamless experience, while Kobo e-readers borrow from OverDrive right on the device and make sideloading easier.

What's the difference between the Kindle and the Kindle Paperwhite?

The Kindle Paperwhite adds a bigger 7-inch screen (vs 6 inches), IPX8 waterproofing, adjustable warm light and up to 12 weeks of battery (vs 6) for about $50 more. Both use the same Kindle store and software, and NBC Select recommends the Paperwhite if you can afford it.

Is a color e-reader worth it?

A color e-reader is worth it only if you read comics, graphic novels, cookbooks or other illustrated books. Color E Ink is half as sharp as black-and-white text (150 vs 300 ppi) and looks muted next to a phone or tablet, and the Kindle Colorsoft costs about $90 more than the Paperwhite.

Can you borrow library books on a Kindle?

Yes, in the US you can borrow library ebooks on a Kindle through the Libby app's Read with Kindle option, with a library card and an Amazon account, though Tom's Guide notes it depends on your library. Kobo e-readers such as the Clara BW borrow library books directly on the device through OverDrive.

When is the best time to buy a Kindle?

The best times to buy a Kindle are Amazon's Prime Day sales and Black Friday, according to TechRadar. Amazon released a new 2026 Kindle, Paperwhite and Colorsoft lineup in October, so this holiday season's deals are on current models.
