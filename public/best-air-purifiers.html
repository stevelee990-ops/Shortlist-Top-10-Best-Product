How to choose

Match CADR to your room

CADR (clean air delivery rate) is how many cubic feet of clean air a purifier puts out per minute. NBC Select and Tom's Guide suggest a CADR of at least two-thirds of the room's square footage, so a 300 sq ft bedroom needs about 200. CADR tests assume 8-ft ceilings, so size up for taller rooms.

Insist on True HEPA

A True HEPA filter captures 99.97% of particles at 0.3 microns, the U.S. Department of Energy standard. 'HEPA-type' or 'HEPA-like' filters aren't certified. AHAM Verified ratings and CARB certification are good signs a maker's claims hold up.

Add carbon for smells and smoke

HEPA filters trap particles such as dust, pollen and dander but do little for odors. For cooking smells, pets or wildfire smoke, look for an activated carbon layer, as on the Blueair Blue Signature, Levoit Vital 200S and Winix 5530.

Budget for filters

Most filters need replacing every 6 to 12 months, so check the price before you buy: Engadget lists about $50 for Levoit Core 400S filters, while the Dyson HushJet's filter lasts up to five years. A washable pre-filter catches hair and helps the main filter last.

Check noise and modes

For bedrooms, choose at least three fan speeds plus a sleep mode; several picks here run at 24 dB or less on low. An auto mode with an air-quality sensor turns the fan up only when the air gets dirty, so you can leave the purifier on all day.

Questions people ask

Do air purifiers really work?

Yes, for airborne particles: a True HEPA filter captures 99.97% of particles at 0.3 microns, and in Tom's Guide's tests the Blueair Blue Signature cleared smoke in about five minutes. They can't remove carbon dioxide or dust already settled on carpets and bedding, so Engadget and Live Science say to keep ventilating and vacuuming.

What size air purifier do I need?

Pick one with a CADR of at least two-thirds of your room's square footage, so a 300 sq ft room needs a CADR of about 200. NBC Select notes AHAM recommends 4.8 air changes per hour (the CDC says 5), and rooms with ceilings above 8 ft need a higher CADR.

Should I run my air purifier all the time?

Yes, leaving it on low or auto mode is the practical approach. Engadget suggests running it on low most of the time and turning it up when the air gets murky, such as after cooking; auto mode does this for you with an air-quality sensor.

Where should I put an air purifier?

Put it in the room where you spend the most time, usually the bedroom, with clear space around it: Engadget suggests about a foot from walls and furniture, with one-sided intakes facing the middle of the room. Live Science adds keeping it away from stoves, fireplaces, humidifiers and damp bathrooms.

What's the best air purifier for allergies?

The Winix 5530 is RTINGS' top pick for allergies in 2026. In general, choose a True HEPA purifier with a high pollen CADR, as Tom's Guide advises, and size it for the bedroom, since Live Science cites EPA-referenced studies suggesting a purifier where you sleep may work better than one in a central spot.

Is an air purifier the same as a humidifier?

No. An air purifier filters particles and odors out of the air, but it doesn't change humidity, as Live Science points out. For dry winter air you need a humidifier, and for damp rooms or mold a dehumidifier, which tackles the moisture an air purifier can't.
